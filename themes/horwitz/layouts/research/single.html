Research

We study the neural mechanisms of visual perception and visually guided behavior in macaque monkeys, focusing on how signals from cone photoreceptors are combined along the LGN–V1 pathway to support spatial and color vision, what limits the perception of flicker, and how visual signals guide eye movements. We combine Neuropixels electrophysiology, behavior, and viral vector–mediated optogenetics.

Data in action: FEF optogenetic stimulation

The animation below shows a memory-guided saccade task (Loki, 2025-01-31). A target briefly appears and is extinguished; the animal must hold it in memory and saccade to it after the fixation point turns off. On laser trials, the frontal eye field (FEF) is stimulated optogenetically during the target-on epoch. Spike trains from four Neuropixels units at different cortical depths show a marked increase in firing during the stimulation window (highlighted in orange) on laser trials compared to the no-laser control.

Data in action: LGN natural foraging

A macaque monkey (Osiris, 2024-01-31) freely views a natural image while a Neuropixels probe records 263 LGN neurons simultaneously. Each neuron has a small receptive field (RF) at a fixed location roughly 15–24° into the upper-right visual field — the probe samples a compact strip of one isoeccentricity band. The display below is in retinal coordinates: the image pans so the fixation point (crosshair) stays centered, as the retina sees it. Each circle marks the RF of one recorded neuron; brightness reflects instantaneous firing rate. When a salient feature sweeps across the RF cluster during a saccade, the entire population lights up briefly.
